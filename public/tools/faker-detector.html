How it works, and where it fails

In many natural lists of numbers that span several sizes, like town populations, about 30% start with 1 and fewer than 5% start with 9. People inventing numbers tend to spread the first digits too evenly. The detector compares your list with 2,000 simulated natural lists of the same size and tells you how unusual yours is.

It is a hint, not proof. It fails on short lists, on data with a narrow range like heights or ages, on rounded numbers, and on anyone who knows the trick. Try to beat it. If you can, you have just learned why a detector's verdict is never the same as the truth.

Every detector has this trade-off. Set the bar low and it catches more fakes but accuses honest lists. OpenAI withdrew its own AI-text classifier in July 2023, citing low accuracy. A Stanford study (Liang et al., arXiv 2304.02819) found that detectors flagged more than half of English essays by non-native writers as AI-written.
