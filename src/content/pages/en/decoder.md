---
title: Decoder
description: Plain-language definitions for the words used in the cases.
---
**Dataset:** a table of information, one row per thing you measured.

**Average (mean):** add everything up and divide by how many there are. One huge value can drag it far from the crowd.

**Median:** line everything up and take the middle one. Much harder to push around.

**Outlier:** a value far away from the rest. It might be an error, or the most interesting row in the file.

**Axis:** the line along the side or bottom of a chart that shows the scale.

**Truncated axis:** an axis that does not start at zero. It can make small differences look huge.

**Cherry-picking:** showing only the part of the data that supports your story.

**Share:** a part as a fraction or percentage of the whole.

**Indicator:** a measured number used to describe something, like income per person.

**Scale:** how the numbers on an axis are spaced.

**Log scale:** a scale where each step is a multiplication, not an addition.

**Country average:** one number summing up a whole country. It hides the differences between people inside it.

**Correlation:** how closely two things move together, from -1 to +1. Moving together does not mean one causes the other.

**Third variable (confounder):** something hidden that pushes two other things at the same time.

**Reverse causation:** when B actually causes A, not the other way round.

**Chance:** a pattern that appears by luck and would not repeat.

**Data dredging:** testing so many pairs that something looks real by chance.

**Bias:** a pattern in how data was collected that makes it unfair or misleading.

**Training:** showing a machine many examples so it can learn a pattern.
